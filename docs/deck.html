Slide 1 · The problem

A Punjab wheat farmer asks:
“What do I plant after wheat — and when?”

Monsoon onset has moved, summers are hotter, and the soil is tired after years of wheat–cotton. The farmer has no data — only memory. A wrong rotation wastes a season of water and soil.

FasalShift answers that question with live NASA Earth observations: it measures how far the growing window has already shifted, then scores three rotations and recommends one — with the reasons visible.

Slide 3 · The Shift — the hero number

+2 days

Faisalabad’s monsoon onset moved 2 days later since 2004

The shift is small — but the heat is not: grain-fill heat days 0 → 5, GDD +265. That is the warming signal inside the same 22 years.

Onset rule (shown in the app): first day after June 1 with a 3-day rain total ≥ 7.5 mm. 2004 onset: June 16. Recent median (2023–2025): June 18.

  • Trend 2004–2025: −0.54 days/year, p = 0.12 — not statistically significant. We report the median shift honestly, not a trend claim.
  • Growing degree days: 2,416 → 2,681 (2004-05 → 2024-25).
  • Grain-fill heat days: 0 → 5 — the warming signal is real even where the onset shift is small.
  • Other districts: Lahore +12 days, Multan −5, Layyah −4.

Weights shown in the app: water 35% · soil 25% · heat 25% · margin 15%. Pulses win on water + soil (they fix nitrogen); cotton wins on heat tolerance but drains soil and water; maize wins on margin but loses on heat.

Inputs (live): 2025 monsoon rain 5.02 mm/day vs 2.37 climatology = 2.12× (wet year). Water scores use a 1.0× scoring cap (labelled rule): surplus rain above climatology cannot differentiate crops, so the wet year scores as 1.0× — the displayed 2.12× is the true ratio. Heat pressure capped at 2.0× because the 2004 baseline grain fill had 0 heat days (verified: max 27.89 °C). Wheat baseline yield 3.12 t/ha is a verified model; rotation yields are labelled relative rules, never model predictions.

Slide 5 · Adaptation Margin — the stress test

2 days

In Faisalabad, every rotation still works if planting slips 2 days — then the heat breaks it

We push planting later, one day at a time, over the real 2024-25 daily series and ask: does the crop still get enough growing-degree days, water, and a survivable grain fill? Faisalabad survives 2 days of slip on all three rotations; Multan survives 8 days; Layyah 3 days; Lahore 2 days. (All-district margins verified from the live cache + scoring engine, Nov 14.)

That is the number a farmer plans around: not a forecast, but the last safe planting day under the weather that already happened.

Slide 6 · Validity + limits (what we do not claim)

Honest about what is live and what is not

  • Live: NASA POWER daily + climatology (verified HTTP 200, Nov 14); rotation scores and margins computed from that data in the app; 42/42 unit tests passing.
  • Not significant: the 22-year onset trend (p = 0.12) — reported, not claimed.
  • Fallbacks, labelled: soil moisture is Open-Meteo/ERA5, not SMAP; monsoon water uses POWER rain, not IMERG.
  • Not built: Landsat/ECOSTRESS processing pipeline (metadata only); no ML rotation model — transparent rules instead.
  • No pilot users yet. No farmer has validated the recommendation in the field; the app says so.
  • Data lag: POWER publishes ~2 days late; the newest days show as “not yet published”, never as zero.
